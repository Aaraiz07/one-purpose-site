Privacy Policy

One Purpose · Last updated 31 October 2026

The short version. One Purpose does not have accounts and does not ask who you are. Everything about your reading — your position in the Quran, your daily goal, your streak — stays on your device and is never uploaded. The app sends anonymous crash reports and usage statistics only if you turn them on in Settings (they are off when you install the app). You can turn them off again at any time.

Who is responsible

One Purpose is published by Muhammad Aaraiz Wasim, an individual developer. This policy covers the iOS app One Purpose. For anything in this policy, contact aaraizwaseem@gmail.com.

What stays on your device

The following never leaves your phone. It is stored in the app's private storage and is removed when you uninstall the app:

  • Your reading position in the Quran
  • Your daily goal, streak, and completion history
  • Your chosen script, font, translation, and language
  • Your reminder time and notification preference

There is no account and no sync. The only server One Purpose operates is a small relay (a Cloudflare Worker) that forwards your requests for Quran text to Quran.Foundation and keeps the answers briefly so they load faster; the relay's own code stores and logs nothing about you.

The Quran text and translations

One Purpose uses the Quran.Foundation APIs (the service behind Quran.com) to download the Arabic text and the translation you choose. Quran data is provided by Quran.Foundation. When you read a surah or change translation, the app asks their servers for that text and keeps a copy on your device so it can be read again without a connection. These requests carry no account or personal details, but, as with any internet request, the servers that answer them (the relay, hosted by Cloudflare, and Quran.Foundation) can see your device's IP address. How Quran.Foundation handles that is described in their privacy policy.

Keeping the service safe

To stop automated abuse of our Quran text relay, the app proves to it that the request comes from a genuine copy of One Purpose on a real Apple device, using Apple's App Attest. This sends a random, anonymous key identifier created on your device and Apple's signed proof, never your name, Apple ID or anything you read. It happens whether or not you enable usage statistics, and the identifier is deleted with the app. The relay also counts how many requests come from each IP address for a few seconds so it can slow down abuse; it does not keep a log of them. The app also checks a small settings file on the relay at launch, which is how we can show a short maintenance notice or ask people to update; that request carries nothing about you.

What is sent off your device

Crash reports off unless you enable them

The app uses Firebase Crashlytics, a service provided by Google, to report crashes so they can be diagnosed and fixed. If you enable this in Settings, a report is sent only when the app actually crashes, and contains:

  • The technical stack trace of the fault
  • Device model, operating system version, and app version
  • A randomly generated installation identifier, which is not linked to you and is reset if you reinstall

Crash reports contain no personal information and nothing about what you were reading.

Usage statistics off unless you enable them

If you enable analytics in Settings, the app uses Firebase Analytics to record anonymous events that show how the app is used in aggregate — for example that a daily goal was completed, that a language was changed, or that a verse was shared.

These events record counts and settings, never content. The app never sends the text of a verse, and there are no free-text fields.

This is off when you install the app. Nothing is sent unless you switch it on, and switching it off stops collection.

Who else sees it

Crash and analytics data is processed by Google as part of Firebase, acting as a service provider on our behalf. It is not sold, and it is not shared with advertisers. One Purpose contains no advertising and no third-party tracking or ad SDKs.

Google's handling of this data is governed by the Firebase Privacy and Security policy.

Optional tips

One Purpose is free. If you choose to leave a tip, the purchase is made through Apple's In-App Purchase and is handled entirely by Apple. We never see your name, card, or Apple ID, only that a tip was completed. A tip unlocks nothing.

Your rights

If you are in the UK, EU, or another region with equivalent law, you have the right to access, correct, or erase data relating to you, and to object to or restrict its processing.

In practice, most of this is directly in your hands:

  • Stop analytics collection — turn the setting off in the app.
  • Erase everything held on your device — uninstall the app.
  • Request erasure of crash or analytics records — email us. Because the data is not linked to your identity, we may not be able to find the records of a particular person, but we will do what we can with details such as your device model and the approximate date.

The lawful basis for both crash reporting and analytics is your consent, which you give by enabling the setting and may withdraw at any time.

Children

One Purpose is suitable for all ages and is not directed at children specifically. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age.

Retention

Crash reports are retained by Firebase Crashlytics for 90 days. Analytics events are retained for no more than 14 months. Data on your device is kept until you delete it or uninstall the app.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the updated version will be posted here with a new date, and a notice will appear in the app's release notes.
